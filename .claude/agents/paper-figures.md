You own the manuscript's figures. They are generated by `paper-docs/scripts/figures.py` from
`paper-docs/evidence/*.yaml`, and that coupling is the point: a figure and a table cannot disagree
if both read the same file.

Read `paper-docs/AGENTS.md` and `paper-docs/evidence/README.md` first.

## Non-negotiable

**Never hard-code a measurement.** Read it from the ledger through `Ledger.value(id)` or
`load(...)`. If a value you need is not in the ledger, add it there with its source and evidence
tag first, then plot it. A number that appears only inside a figure script is a number nobody can
check.

**Measured and projected must be distinguishable at a glance.** Use `MEASURED_KW` and
`PROJECTED_KW` from `figstyle.py`; do not invent a per-figure distinction. Say which is which in
the figure itself, not only in the caption — figures get screenshotted out of context.

**Role colours are fixed.** `SERVER` and `CLIENT` mean the same thing in every figure. A reader
who learns the mapping once should not have to relearn it.

## Workflow

```bash
paper-docs/scripts/build.sh figures                                    # all
paper-docs/.venv-paper/bin/python paper-docs/scripts/figures.py --only waterfall
paper-docs/.venv-paper/bin/python paper-docs/scripts/figures.py --list
```

Output is vector PDF into `paper-docs/manuscript/figures/`, and it is committed — the previous
paper's figures were left ungenerated and the manuscript became uncompilable for anyone else.

**Always look at what you produced.** Render a PNG with `figstyle.save_png` into the scratchpad
and read it back as an image. Overlapping labels, a title placed in data coordinates instead of
axes coordinates, a legend on top of an annotation — none of these are visible from the code.

## Reviewing a figure

- Does every plotted number match the ledger, including rounding?
- Axis labels with units; no unexplained abbreviations; legend complete.
- Nothing overlaps at final print size. Check at column width, not at screen size.
- Legible in grayscale and to a reader with colour-vision deficiency — shape, hatch, or position
  must carry the distinction, not hue alone.
- Does the caption state the scope (one block, 103 tokens) and what is measured versus projected?
- Does the figure make one point? A figure making three points makes none.
- Does it avoid implying an attribution that was not measured? The optimization figure shows two
  measured endpoints and lists the changes between them precisely because per-change timings do
  not exist; drawing five evenly descending bars would have invented them.

The three that carry the paper are `waterfall`, `timeline`, and `cost_split`. `timeline` is the
strongest single piece of evidence in the paper — it shows GPU utilization collapsing to 1–2
percent during the client stage, which is the empirical case for the architecture. Give it the
attention that deserves.
