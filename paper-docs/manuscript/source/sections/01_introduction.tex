\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Human genomic sequence carries an unusually persistent confidentiality risk. It can identify its
contributor and, through familial relationships, reveal information about people who never supplied
a sample; re-identification has been demonstrated even after conventional identifiers were
removed~\cite{gymrek2013,erlich2018}. A disclosed genome also cannot be replaced like a password or
access token. European data-protection law accordingly treats genetic data as a special
category~\cite{gdpr}. Genomic foundation models derive their value from that same sequence. DNAGPT
supports classification, regression, and generation through a common transformer
backbone~\cite{dnagpt2023}; the downstream tasks evaluated here use human sequence. When such a
model is offered only as a service, conventional inference requires the data owner to send the
sequence or a representation of it to the provider. Plaintext embeddings do not necessarily remove
the risk: per-token representations from DNA foundation models can permit near-perfect sequence
reconstruction~\cite{dnaembeddinginversion2026}. Homomorphic encryption offers a way for the
provider to compute without receiving those genomic values in plaintext.

DNAGPT is publicly available and can be run locally; public weights make the encrypted computation
auditable. The deployment studied here represents a provider that retains a genomic model
server-side for commercial, licensing, or governance reasons. In that setting, the data owner must
either disclose the genomic input, forgo the inference, or use a privacy-preserving protocol. We
therefore ask whether a complete DNAGPT transformer can be evaluated under homomorphic encryption,
and what limits its numerical fidelity, memory use, and runtime. Feasibility asks whether the
complete computation executes within the cryptographic and memory budget while meeting a
predeclared numerical criterion. Practicality asks whether the resulting cost suits the intended
deployment.

CKKS supports the packed additions, multiplications, and rotations used by transformer linear
layers and attention products~\cite{ckks2017}, but LayerNorm, softmax, and GELU require functions
that are expensive to evaluate homomorphically. A fully non-interactive design must approximate
them with polynomial circuits and provide a larger depth and refresh budget. In our evaluated
non-interactive configuration, one transformer block completes, but multi-block composition
exceeds the tested memory envelope. This motivates the client-assisted protocol evaluated here:
the data owner evaluates the three nonlinear functions exactly at predetermined boundaries and
returns fresh ciphertexts. The refreshes reset the multiplicative-depth budget and avoid
homomorphic bootstrapping in the retained protocol. Client-assisted nonlinear evaluation is
established prior art~\cite{thex2022,safhire2025}; our study measures its numerical behavior,
memory, interaction, and executed work for a complete genomic transformer whose weights and
nonlinear formulas remain unchanged.

We first reproduce DNAGPT on genomic-signal recognition, promoter and splice-site classification,
and mRNA abundance regression. For numerical validation, we implement the forward computation
independently in PyTorch float64 and NumPy float64 and compare their outputs after every transformer
block and the task head. Once these implementations agree, the NumPy output becomes the plaintext
reference for encrypted evaluation. We then evaluate non-interactive and client-assisted CKKS
designs under checked operation schedules.

The client-assisted protocol executes all twelve transformer blocks and the task head at the
$103$-token benchmark while maintaining a maximum multiplicative depth of $13$ and bounded
accelerator memory. Its output satisfies the predeclared numerical tolerance by several orders of
magnitude. The complete execution takes $1.86$~hours on one A100, making runtime instead of
correctness or accelerator capacity the main limitation of the current implementation.

The paper makes the following contributions:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Complete-model graph feasibility.} All twelve DNAGPT transformer blocks and the
        task head execute at the $103$-token maximum encrypted-scope classification benchmark from
        encrypted embedded vectors, with the original weights and nonlinear formulas and without
        homomorphic bootstrapping. To our knowledge this is the first complete CKKS forward pass of
        a pretrained genomic foundation model; prior homomorphic transformer evaluations over
        biological sequence data have targeted small, purpose-trained
        classifiers~\cite{idash24descriptor,encrypteddashformer}.
  \item \textbf{Independently checked numerical evaluation.} The protocol, packing, checked
        operation schedule, predeclared tolerance, and validation chain make the complete execution
        independently inspectable.
  \item \textbf{Measured resource behavior.} We report complete-model cost, interaction,
        multiplicative depth, and memory; the stage-bounded cache that makes host-side reuse
        executable; and the memory boundary reached by the evaluated non-interactive configuration.
\end{itemize}
