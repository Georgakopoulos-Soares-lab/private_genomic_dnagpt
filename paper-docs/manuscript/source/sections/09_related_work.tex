\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{Private transformer inference}

Private neural-network inference has long combined cryptographic tools according to the
operation being evaluated. Gazelle established an influential hybrid design in which
homomorphic encryption handles linear layers and garbled circuits handle
nonlinearities~\cite{gazelle2018}. Transformer systems inherited this decomposition but had
to contend with attention, normalization, and activation functions at substantially greater
scale. BOLT, Iron, Nimbus, and BumbleBee combine homomorphic encryption with secret sharing,
oblivious transfer, and specialized two-party protocols for transformer
inference~\cite{bolt2024,iron2022,nimbus2024,bumblebee2025}. Their protocols optimize a joint secure
computation between parties; the present work instead keeps the server's linear path in CKKS
and assigns exact nonlinear evaluation to the data owner at explicit boundaries. This choice
changes both the security contract and the allocation of work between the parties.

The client-aided division of labor predates transformer work. nGraph-HE2 evaluates standard
pre-trained networks under CKKS while returning to the client to compute their native activation
functions, precisely so that those activations need not be replaced by polynomials~\cite{ngraphhe2},
and THE-X carries the pattern to transformers: the server evaluates HE-compatible operations and the
client decrypts and re-encrypts around nonlinear functions~\cite{thex2022}. Safhire is the closest
architectural antecedent~\cite{safhire2025}: it likewise places homomorphically evaluated linear
layers on the server and has the client decrypt an intermediate, apply the nonlinearity in
plaintext, and return a fresh encryption, though it studies convolutional networks and adds
randomized output permutation to make reconstruction from released intermediates harder. The
client-assisted pattern is thus established prior art, and we adopt rather than propose it. What
this study adds is its application to a complete forward pass of a released genomic transformer at
that model's task-defined prompt, preserving the released weights and exact nonlinearities, together
with an independently checked plaintext reference that makes the resulting arithmetic, boundary
schedule, and memory behavior reproducible.

The non-interactive line of work supplies useful cost context. THOR evaluates a $12$-layer,
$768$-wide encoder at $128$ tokens on a single A100, at ring degree $2^{16}$, depth $13$, and
$128$-bit security, in $602$~s end to end, with no client interaction at any
point~\cite{thor}. NEXUS reaches non-interactivity over RNS-CKKS with SIMD ciphertext compression
and a secure argmax~\cite{nexus2025}; its headline per-inference figure is amortized over a batch
and its single-prediction latency is substantially higher, so the two readings should not be
conflated. MOAI reports a further $52.8\%$ reduction in evaluation time against THOR by holding one
packing convention across all layers and removing rotations from softmax and
normalization~\cite{moai2025}. Powerformer instead modifies the model, training HE-suitable
polynomial components rather than preserving a released transformer unchanged~\cite{powerformer2025},
and ELLMo treats packing and depth as coupled design choices~\cite{ellmo2026}.

Model semantics, prompt length, nonlinear circuits, bootstrapping, packing, libraries, CPU paths,
and measurement endpoints differ across these systems, so they establish that the present
implementation is not performance-leading without isolating the cost of client assistance. The
evaluated design occupies a different point in that space: it retains the released nonlinear
formulas exactly, requires no homomorphic bootstrap, and pairs its execution with a frozen,
independently checked plaintext reference.

\subsection{Homomorphic encryption for genomics}

Homomorphic encryption already has a substantial genomic-computing lineage. The iDASH secure
genome analysis competition evaluated encrypted association studies, sequence search, and
related privacy-preserving workflows~\cite{idash2018}. Private genomic-query systems combine
homomorphic encryption with hashing and set-intersection techniques to test for variants
without disclosing either the stored genome or the query~\cite{privategenomicqueries2017}.
Those workloads center on searches or prescribed statistics over genomic records. DNAGPT inference
instead composes dense projections, attention, normalization, and a feed-forward network while
preserving the numerical behavior of a released foundation model. This study extends the genomic
HE lineage to the feasibility boundary of that transformer forward pass.

The iDASH series has since moved from encrypted statistics toward encrypted neural evaluation, and
supplies the closest prior art to this study. Its 2024 homomorphic-encryption track distributed
DASHformer, a protein-family classifier comprising a tokenizer, an encoder, a single transformer
block with four-head self-attention, and a dense classification layer, totaling $138{,}905$
parameters over $50$-residue fragments~\cite{idash24descriptor}. The winning solution evaluates it
non-interactively under leveled CKKS, with no bootstrapping and no client participation at any
point, classifying a batch of $163$ encrypted sequences in about $165$~s at $128$-bit
security~\cite{encrypteddashformer}. On interaction that result is strictly stronger than the
design evaluated here, which returns to the key holder at every nonlinearity. It reaches that
strength under different constraints: one transformer block against twelve, a purpose-trained
competition classifier against a released $0.1$-billion-parameter backbone, and nonlinearities
replaced by data-driven non-polynomial fits rather than preserved. To our knowledge, no released,
pretrained genomic foundation model has previously been evaluated under homomorphic encryption for
a complete forward pass.

\subsection{GPU acceleration and homomorphic matrix primitives}

GPU CKKS libraries move number-theoretic transforms, key switching, rotations, and
ciphertext arithmetic onto accelerators; the evaluated backend supplies those primitives and
interoperates with the host cryptographic runtime~\cite{gpu-ckks-backend,openfhe}. Its API also
shaped the available systems designs: the absence of ciphertext serialization prevented
process-sharded evaluation, leaving thread-level concurrency and in-process device placement as the
implementable paths. Recent multi-GPU transformer work treats placement and
communication--computation overlap as first-class scheduling problems~\cite{aegis}, but the current
implementation neither serializes ciphertexts between processes nor exercises a networked transport,
so those paths must be evaluated directly before adopting a multi-device schedule.

Matrix layout determines which rotations, plaintext encodings, and reductions an encrypted
linear layer requires. THOR's diagonal-major representation and compact packing specialize
that layout for transformer inference~\cite{thor}; HE-BLAS develops a broader library of
homomorphic linear-algebra kernels and shape-aware algorithms~\cite{heblas2025}. These works
motivate the remaining targets in Section~\ref{sec:optimization}, as does MOAI's rotation-free
formulation of softmax and normalization~\cite{moai2025}: reducing repeated packing work, copying
fewer distinct diagonal plaintexts, and replacing segmented slot reductions with kernels matched to
the tensor shapes. Because packing, key material, level consumption, and the operation schedule
interact, each candidate primitive must be revalidated against the frozen plaintext reference and
checked schedule before its performance is compared.
