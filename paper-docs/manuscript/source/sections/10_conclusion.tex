\section{Conclusion}

This study gives a split answer to the question of encrypted DNAGPT inference. Client-assisted CKKS
evaluates the complete released model, twelve blocks and the task head at the task's own prompt
length, and reproduces the frozen plaintext label with $8.56\times10^{-9}$ relative margin error
against a tolerance of $4\times10^{-2}$ fixed before the run. Correctness is therefore not the
barrier, and approximation error does not accumulate to a decision-changing level across depth.
Memory did prevent chained evaluation in the evaluated non-interactive configuration. Explicit
re-encryption removes that accelerator-memory barrier rather than postponing it: depth is bounded
by the longest segment between client boundaries, GPU memory stays at $9839$~MiB with no growth
across the twelve blocks, and the model requires no homomorphic bootstrap. Bounded plaintext caching addresses
a separate host-memory problem by releasing encoded templates at stage boundaries. The feasibility
verdict is affirmative for a complete model.

Cost is the constraint that remains. One encrypted classification of one $600$-base-pair sequence
was measured at $1.86$~hours on an A100 GPU node, which forecloses interactive per-query use while
leaving batch or offline analysis available where disclosing the sequence is unacceptable. That
figure comes from a single execution and describes one inference rather than a service rate. It is
also not a cryptographic floor: GPU utilization never exceeds $51\%$, so the cost sits in
host-side encoding and the sequential client boundary, both of which host-side systems work
shows to be addressable on an unchanged circuit. Nor is it competitive: non-interactive systems
report an encrypted forward pass of a comparable encoder at these parameters and on this accelerator
roughly an order of magnitude faster, and without an online client. Feasibility and practicality
consequently have different verdicts, and the honest answer to the deployment question is a correct,
exactly reproduced encrypted path that is currently neither interactive nor the cheapest encrypted
route to a prediction.

\subsection*{Future work}

The immediate measurement priority is to repeat the complete-model run, so that its cost is
supported by a distribution rather than by one sample, and to pair it with a dedicated-node
measurement of the documented pre-optimization baseline. Systems work should then cache mask
plaintexts, extending the encode-once strategy
already validated for weight diagonals. Distinct transforms across the packed activation copies are
the larger circuit opportunity; before mask and copy-repair overhead, the derived schedule for that
layout projects a reduction of roughly $60\%$ in the block's total ciphertext--plaintext count. A thread-safe cryptographic context would permit
concurrent independent client boundaries. Protocol closure also requires a noise-flooding budget
for re-encryption. A deployment study must finally add serialized network transport and private
token-index lookup before measuring the complete service.

\section*{Ethics statement}

This study involved no human participants, specimens, or private clinical records. It used public
reference datasets with recorded provenance. The work makes no clinical-performance claim and
does not present the prototype as a deployable privacy guarantee beyond its stated threat model.

\section*{Data availability statement}

All evaluation datasets are public. Their original sources, versions, preprocessing, and any
recovery route used are recorded in the repository's data-provenance document. Sequence data,
model weights, and cryptographic keys are not redistributed.

\section*{Code availability statement}

The evaluation harnesses, encrypted-evaluation implementation, environment definitions, and the
evidence record for the paper are maintained at
\url{https://github.com/Georgakopoulos-Soares-lab/private_genomic_dnagpt}. No repository-level
reuse license has yet been assigned.
